# Waiting for better models

The restart did not move at a steady pace. It went in bursts, and the
quiet stretches between them were not holidays. Twice the work reached a
point the model of the day could not get past, and twice it started again
when a new Claude model arrived. The prompt history shows it plainly.

## Spring: close, and stuck

The first push, in March, ran on Claude Opus 4.6 with the effort setting
at its maximum. It went fast at first. In a fortnight every version was
disassembled and decompiled, Rogue 3.4 was read through function by
function, and a rebuild of Rogue 5.4's object file was under way. On
2 April that rebuild came within two small patches of matching.

Then it stopped converging. The last differences in 5.4 resisted
everything, and the model kept concluding that what was left could not be
fixed, or was close enough. The person running the work kept insisting it
could be, and that "close enough" was not the standard. By the end of
March it was stuck all the same. In mid-April the effort
setting was turned down, turned back up, and then the work stopped. For
seven weeks there was almost nothing.

## June: a new model, and then the right one

Work started again on 9 June with a different approach: instead of
reading Rogue 3.4 harder, build the 2.8BSD compiler and prove each
function by compiling it. Claude Opus 4.8 appears in the commits the next
day, and within two days the C library, curses and termcap libraries all
matched the binary exactly.

On 12 June Claude Fable 5 came out, and the problems that had been set
aside were handed to it the same day. The next week was the breakthrough
of the whole project: the remaining differences in Rogue 3.4 were worked
through one after another, with the effort setting raised again when a
problem needed it.

About a week later Fable was taken away for a while, and the work waited
for it. For two weeks at the end of June there are almost no prompts at
all.

## July: everything falls

When it came back, things fell quickly. Rogue 3.4 matched byte for byte
on 3 July. In the next three weeks so did Rogue 3.6 on both machines,
4.22, 5.3 and 5.2, and the [toolchains](toolchains.md) and
[simulators](simulators.md) grew into the general-purpose tools they are now.

## November: the ports

Then another quiet stretch, from September to November. The Linux ports
had started, and the Rogue 5.2 port had been stuck for a while. Claude
Fable 5.2 was first used on 11 November and Claude Opus 5.5 on
14 November, and in the week that followed all six Berkeley versions were ported, with
more than five hundred separate bug fixes between them, and PC Rogue
after them.

## What still waits

Two spots in Rogue 5.4 are still patched by hand after compiling: the
exit from `do_move()` and six bytes of register choice in `command()`.
The likely cause is a compiler build slightly different from the one
reconstructed. If the pattern holds, they are waiting for the next model.
